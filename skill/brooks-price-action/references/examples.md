# Worked examples (paraphrased from Brooks Trading Course posts; personal-study notes)

These show the *shape* of a good reading. They are condensed paraphrases, not quotations.

## 1. Weekly chart — pullback to the EMA inside a bull trend (S&P E-mini, week ending 2026-10-24)

- **Last bar:** bear bar closing near its low, prominent upper tail, closing around the 20-week EMA. Recall what
  last week's report said traders would watch and whether it happened.
- **Bulls see:** the last two weeks as a pullback forming a double-bottom bull flag (Oct 1 / Oct 23); the
  consecutive bear bars as just a leg inside a small trading range. **Want:** the 20-week EMA or the Sep 16 low to
  hold as support; a third leg up completing an embedded wedge (Jun 1, Aug 13 = legs 1–2); a measured move to ~8000
  (spike Mar 30 low → Apr 17 high projected). **Need:** consecutive strong bull bars for trend resumption.
  **Fallback:** if lower, the Jul 29 low area as support.
- **Bears see:** a wedge top (Dec 6, Oct 29, Aug 13) plus an embedded wedge; the sideways range as a possible
  *final flag*. **Hope:** the large bear bars flipped the market Always In Short. **Need:** strong follow-through
  selling below the EMA and the Sep 16 low. **If higher:** Oct 21 high as resistance → small double-top bear flag;
  they want any rally weak (overlap, upper tails, no follow-through).
- **Synthesis:** "Traders are wondering whether two bear bars are enough to flip to Always In Short — more likely
  only if next week has follow-through."
- **Traders will watch:** follow-through below EMA + Sep 16 low vs. support there and a retest of the high.

Lesson: no verdict yet — the reading names the exact evidence each side needs and the dated levels.

## 2. Daily chart of the same week — 58-day trading range

- Day-by-day description (tested the 20-day EMA, then gapped down Thursday → bear bar mid-close with lower tail,
  Friday gap down → doji with upper tail).
- Context: a 58-day sideways range near the all-time high; bears call it a possible final flag of the rally and
  want a double top with the Aug 13 high; bulls see a two-legged sideways-to-down pullback (DB bull flag
  Oct 1 / Oct 23).
- Strength nuance: no consecutive bear bars, but a gap and a micro gap on the way down = *some* bear strength.
- Both sides' "want the move to be weak" descriptions: overlapping candles, tails, opposite-colour bars.

Lesson: strength is graded (consecutive bars, closes, gaps, overlap), not binary.

## 3. Pre-open daily report written by Al (Emini, 2019-06-27)

- Fourth consecutive bear day reversing down from a double top; the 20-day EMA and a tight range below are
  **magnets**; momentum is strong so it should reach them within a week — *but* this is still more likely a
  **pullback in a bull trend** than a new bear trend.
- Overnight up a few points → "75% chance of a bull breakout above the bear channel, then a couple hours sideways
  to up." Odds of another all-day selloff are lower because 4 consecutive bear days in a bull trend are usually a
  pullback that lasts a few days.
- EURUSD daily: two inside days = **ii Breakout Mode**, both a buy and sell setup. Bears see a failed breakout
  above a double top and a wedge rally; most traders expect higher; bears still have ~40% — rising if a micro
  double top with a strong bear reversal bar or a bear surprise bar appears.
- Weekly/monthly context at end of day: Friday close matters for the weekly bar (doji vs bear inside bar); the
  monthly bar is an **oo** buy signal bar.

Lesson: causal chain "Since… → probability → Therefore… → However…", always with the minority-side odds.

## 4. Intraday 5-minute summary (Emini, 2026-11-13)

- Day type label: *climactic bear trend from the open, then broad bull channel reversal*.
- Bars are numbered from the open: bars 1–3 strong bear bars → Always In Short on the close of bar 2; the selloff
  to bar 9 was strong but climactic → raises odds of sideways; "if the market gets back to the bar 12 low, buyers
  likely."
- Setups chart: buy entries marked green, sells red, as reasonable **stop entries** from an Always In perspective.
- Trade-location logic: selling below a bar that is far from the rising EMA means selling into an area where bulls
  buy and bears take profits; climactic bars at targets carry reversal risk, while pullbacks/pause bars give better
  entries with closer stops.

Lesson: intraday reads hinge on numbered bars, Always In flips, and location of entries relative to EMA/targets.
